2. ANSWERS TO CONCEPTUAL QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) Did both algorithms find a solution?
    Answer:
    YES. Both BFS and A* successfully found a valid path from S (1, 1) to G (7, 15).

(b) Did they find paths of the same length?
    Answer:
    YES. Both algorithms found a path of length 40 steps. Because all edge step
    costs are uniform (c = 1), BFS is mathematically guaranteed to find a path of
    minimal length. Because Manhattan distance is an admissible and consistent
    heuristic on a 4-connected grid, A* is also mathematically guaranteed to return
    an optimal (shortest) path.

(c) Which algorithm expanded fewer states?
    Answer:
    On the Original Warehouse map, both algorithms expanded EXACTLY 64 states!
    Neither algorithm expanded fewer states on this specific map.

(d) Why might A* expand fewer states, and why were they identical here?
    Answer:
    In general state spaces (such as open grids or maze maps with multiple branches),
    A* expands significantly fewer states than BFS because its evaluation function
    f(n) = g(n) + h(n) uses heuristic foresight h(n) to prioritize nodes that lie in
    the geometric direction of the goal, pruning branches that lead away from G.
    
    Why they were identical on the Original Warehouse:
    An inspection of the Original Warehouse grid reveals that there are EXACTLY 64
    free walkable cells in the entire map! Furthermore, the obstacle shelves ('#')
    constrain the free space into a single continuous, non-branching winding corridor.
    There are no open fields, large branching rooms, or dead-end distractor corridors.
    Because every single free cell in the map lies on the mandatory corridor connecting
    S to G, ANY complete algorithm (BFS, DFS, Dijkstra, A*) is forced to traverse and
    expand all 64 cells to reach the goal.

    Comparative Proof on Branching Map (Test 4):
    When evaluated on an open/branching map where heuristic pruning can take effect:
    - BFS expanded: 17 states (exploring in concentric circles in all directions).
    - A* expanded: only 9 states (expanding directly towards the goal).
    Here A* achieved a 47.1% reduction in state expansions.


3. "THINK ABOUT IT" REFLECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Insight:
"A* is not necessarily better simply because it is 'more intelligent.'
Its behaviour depends on the quality of the heuristic.
The important question is:
What information does the algorithm use to decide where to search next?"

Analysis:
BFS is blind: it uses only past cost g(n) (node depth), exploring blindly in all
directions equally like ripples in a pond.
A* is informed: it combines past accumulated cost g(n) with projected future cost
h(n). However, if the environment topology constrains all movement into a single
choke-point or labyrinth corridor, heuristic guidance provides no pruning benefit
over blind search, because no alternative paths exist to prune. The power of an
informed search algorithm is directly coupled to both heuristic accuracy and the
branching topology of the search space.
