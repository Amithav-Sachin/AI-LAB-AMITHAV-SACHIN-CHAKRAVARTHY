What parameter.grad represents in terms of dL/dW^(1):
By the chain rule of reverse-mode automatic differentiation:
    dL / dW_ij^(1) = (1/N) * sum_{n=1}^N delta_{n, i}^(1) * x_{n, j}
where delta_{n, i}^(1) = (dL_n / da_{n, i}^(1)) = (dL_n / dz_n) * W_i^(2) * sigma'(a_{n, i}^(1)).

Why the gradient is the average of example-wise gradients:
The empirical loss function across the mini-batch is defined as the arithmetic mean:
    L = (1 / N) * sum_{n=1}^N L_n(y_n, y_hat_n)
By linearity of the differentiation operator:
    grad_W L = grad_W [ (1 / N) * sum_{n=1}^N L_n ] = (1 / N) * sum_{n=1}^N grad_W L_n
Thus, the total gradient tensor populated by PyTorch is the exact arithmetic mean
of the four individual example gradients.

THINK ABOUT IT: DISTINGUISHING SIGMOID SATURATION FROM DEAD RELU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question:
"If a sigmoid unit is saturated, its derivative is close to zero. If a ReLU unit is
negative, its derivative is zero. These are different mechanisms that can both
produce a small gradient. How would you distinguish them by inspecting activations
and pre-activations?"

Answer:
To distinguish them, inspect the pre-activation a = W*x + b and activation h = f(a):
- Sigmoid Saturation:
  Examine pre-activation |a|. If |a| >> 0 (e.g., a > 4.0 or a < -4.0), the unit is
  saturated. The activation h = sigma(a) is extremely close to 1.0 or 0.0, and the
  derivative sigma'(a) = h*(1-h) is near zero.
- ReLU Inactivity (Dead ReLU):
  Examine the sign of pre-activation a. If a < 0, the activation is exactly h = 0.0,
  and the sub-gradient is identically 0. If a >= 0, the derivative is 1.0.
Diagnostic Rule:
If h = 0 and a < 0, the unit is in the dead ReLU regime.
If |a| is large and h in (0, 0.02) or (0.98, 1.0), the unit is saturated sigmoid.
