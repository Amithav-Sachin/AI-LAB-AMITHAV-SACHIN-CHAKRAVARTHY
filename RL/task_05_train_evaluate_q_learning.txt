4. REFLECTION QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) Why might the training curve remain noisy after a good route is learned?
    Answer:
    The training curve remains noisy because epsilon-greedy action selection forces
    the agent to take random exploratory actions with probability epsilon throughout
    training. Even when the Q-table has converged to optimal values, whenever the
    exploration coin flip occurs (e.g., with epsilon = 0.05 late in training, or higher
    earlier), the agent chooses an action uniformly at random. This exploratory move
    can slam into an obstacle (-1 penalty) or wander away from the doorway, causing
    occasional low-return episodes or timeouts. Thus, training return reflects the
    performance of an exploratory policy, not the pure greedy policy.

(b) Does a high training return necessarily imply a good greedy policy?
    Answer:
    Not necessarily. A high average training return indicates that the agent often
    accumulates rewards, but it can be misleading:
    1. Exploration noise: An agent might achieve decent training returns through
       lucky exploratory sequences while its underlying greedy policy has local cycles
       or unvisited bottleneck states.
    2. Overestimation bias: Q-learning's maximization step (max_{a'} Q(s', a'))
       can overestimate action values, making suboptimal paths appear lucrative.
    3. Sparse coverage: Only evaluating the policy with epsilon=0 and updates
       disabled reveals whether the greedy actions alone form a coherent, cycle-free,
       optimal trajectory from start to goal.

(c) What is the difference between the learning rate alpha and the exploration
    probability epsilon?
    Answer:
    - Exploration probability (epsilon):
      Controls ACTION SELECTION (Behavior Policy). It dictates how often the agent
      deviates from its current best estimates to try random actions (exploration)
      versus exploiting known high-value actions. It operates in the action space.
    - Learning rate (alpha):
      Controls VALUE ESTIMATION (Temporal Difference Update). It determines the step
      size by which newly observed TD targets adjust existing Q-value estimates:
          Q <- Q + alpha * (Target - Q).
      It controls the retention of old knowledge versus the adoption of new empirical
      evidence. It operates in the parameter / value space.

5. OPTIONAL INVESTIGATION: TRAINING WITH NO EXPLORATION (EPSILON = 0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We investigated training a fresh Q-table with epsilon = 0.0 throughout all 1,500
episodes, tested across multiple random seeds:

Results:
- Seed 7:  Goal reached = True, Path length = 4 steps (Optimal)
- Seed 42: Goal reached = True, Path length = 4 steps (Optimal)
- Seed 99: Goal reached = True, Path length = 4 steps (Optimal)

Explanation of the Phenomenon:
Why did zero exploration succeed rather than immediately failing?
Because all Q-table entries are initialized to 0.0. When all Q-values in a state are
tied at 0.0, the training action selector breaks ties randomly. This random tie-breaking
acts as implicit stochastic exploration during the earliest episodes until rewards are
discovered! Once the goal (+10) is stumbled upon, non-zero positive values propagate
backwards, naturally breaking ties in favor of the optimal path.

However, if tie-breaking during training is made strictly deterministic (e.g., always
choosing the lowest action index 0 = Up when Q-values are tied), the agent with
epsilon = 0 immediately gets trapped: from (2, 0), it takes action Up to (1, 0), then
Up to (0, 0), where it repeatedly attempts Up against the ceiling wall forever,
timing out on every single episode and learning nothing.
This confirms the PDF's observation: "Do not assume that no exploration must fail:
random tie-breaking and initial values can also affect the actions tried."
